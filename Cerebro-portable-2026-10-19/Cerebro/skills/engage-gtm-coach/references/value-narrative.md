## 1. The Returns Challenge

Returns have a 1-to-1 impact on profits — and they're growing with omnichannel expansion.

**Scale of the Problem (2025 data):**
- $706B in total returns
- $100B in preventable loss (14.2% of returns involve fraud and abuse)
- $211B in processing costs (~30% of item value per return)

Source: 2026 Total Retail Loss Benchmark Report, Appriss Retail

### Returns Drive Loyalty — And Risk

Customers care deeply about the return experience, making blanket policies a dangerous gamble:
- 75% of customers factor ease of return into purchase decisions
- 72% shopped elsewhere after a negative return experience
- 83% say a positive return experience boosts intent to buy again
- 47% have refrained from a purchase due to return policy concerns

### The Top 1% Problem

Up to 50% of total sales can come from the top 1% of customers. They return 8% less than average — but blanket policies catch them most often because they buy more. Treating best customers the same as worst is a margin and loyalty risk that behavioral intelligence solves.

### Where Fraud and Abuse Actually Hide

Most abusers don't know they're abusing — they're operating within what they perceive as acceptable behavior. The financial exposure breaks into two categories:

**Abuse | 12% of returns | $86B**
Excessive but legitimate returns. Bracketing, wardrobing, and serial returning — behaviors customers often don't realize are problematic for a retailer's bottom line. Consumers comfortable with gray-area behaviors: 63% return items without tags, 44% buy multiple sizes with intent to return, 40% return used merchandise.

**Fraud | 2% of returns | $14B**
Fake receipts, returning stolen merchandise, and other behaviors with malicious intent. $11B in-store, $3B online.

**The omnichannel gap:** A customer blocked online can return the same item in-store with no warning. Criminals simply move between channels. Receipts don't equal safety — fraudsters are increasingly manipulating returns using AI and social engineering.

**The critical takeaway:** The $86B abuse category — not the $14B fraud category — is where the bulk of preventable loss lives. This is the behavioral middle that blanket policies overlook and rules-based systems can't address. It's also where Engage creates outsized value.

**Coaching Note:** When presenting the returns challenge, don't dwell on all three stat boxes. Hit the $706B to establish scale, then move quickly to the $86B abuse category and the omnichannel gap. That's where the "why Engage" conversation begins.

## 2. The Engage Story

**Every return is a decision point.** Approve it, and you either absorb preventable loss or protect a valuable customer relationship. Deny it, and you either risk giving a good customer a negative brand experience or stop fraud. The difference between those two outcomes comes down to one thing: intelligence.

Appriss Engage is the returns and claims management platform trusted by leading retailers across every major category. It analyzes transactions across stores, websites, and customer service centers and instantly recommends whether to approve, warn, or deny — so your team can act with confidence while building loyalty.

**The tagline:** Stop Fraud and Abuse Without Turning Away Good Customers.

### How Appriss' Intelligence Works

The Appriss Data Intelligence layer unifies store, online, and customer service center data to give retailers a complete, 360 customer-level view of both risk and opportunity. Four-step process:

1. **Ingest** — Store, online, and customer service center data flows into Appriss
2. **Link** — Appriss' Data Intelligence unifies all customer-level data points and identity traits across channels
3. **Analyze** — Advanced machine learning models assess risk in real time, drawing on 20+ years of behavioral data across retailers
4. **Decide** — Approve, warn, or deny so your team can act with confidence in real-time

### The Three-Tier Decision Model

A three-tier approach that educates before it punishes — preserving relationships while reducing abuse:

- **APPROVE:** The vast majority of returns are approved seamlessly. Low-risk and loyal customers proceed without friction. No disruption.
- **WARN:** High-risk patterns trigger a behavioral notification, giving customers a chance to course correct. Importantly, 90% of warned customers come back to buy again.
- **DECLINE:** Only ~1-2% of returns are declined for those meeting clear fraud or extreme abuse thresholds. Think receipt manipulation, organized crime rings, identity fraud.

### Key Capabilities

With Engage, retailers can:
- Keep trusted customers coming back — behavior-based authorization that rewards loyalty instead of punishing it
- Stop fraud and abuse with data — the Appriss Data Intelligence layer unifies store, online, and customer service center data for a complete customer-level view of risk
- Eliminate friction in employee and customer service center decisions — remove guesswork from employee decisions with instant, AI-powered outcomes

### Channel-Specific Use Cases

**In-Store Return Authorization:**
- Receipted and non-receipted return fraud and abuse mitigation

- Associate-level consistency — eliminates subjective judgment calls

**Online Return & Claim Authorization:**

## 3. Why Engage Is Different

The returns management space is fragmented. Most solutions approach it from a single angle — either upstream through checkout fraud prevention, or with manual rule-based policies that lack personalization and real-time intelligence. Appriss Engage operates differently because it was built differently.

### 3a. Why Retailers Can't Build This Themselves

Three reasons:

**The Data Gap:** No single retailer can see the full picture. Their data stops at their own doors. Engage is different — models are trained on consortium data spanning 250M+ unique customer identifiers, 20+ years of retail intelligence, and 40% of all U.S. retail transactions.

**The AI Gap:** Building a returns decisioning engine isn't a rules problem — it's a machine learning problem. Appriss has 12+ AI systems live, trained on billions of labeled retail transactions. Models learn and improve continuously. Replicating that depth internally would require dedicated data science, engineering, and ongoing investment most retailers aren't staffed to sustain.

**AI-Native for 20+ Years:** Appriss didn't bolt AI onto an existing tool. The platform was designed from the beginning to make real-time decisions that authorize returns, stop fraudulent claims, resolve consumer identities across channels, and embed intelligence directly into workflows.

**The Maintenance Gap:** Fraud continually evolves. Abuse patterns shift. Policy changes ripple across channels. A build-your-own solution locks you into a static approach that degrades over time. Engage adapts because the models retrain, the consortium data refreshes, and the intelligence layer keeps pace with changing behavior.

**Bottom line:** Building in-house means investing significant capital to build something narrower, slower, and less accurate than what Engage already delivers. The consortium data moat alone makes replication impractical.

### 3b. How Engage Competes

**Returns Management (Narvar, Loop, parcelLab):**
- What they do: Post-purchase CX — tracking, notifications, returns orchestration
- What they miss: Visibility limited to ecomm-only transaction data. No (or limited) consortium data. Overreliance on rules-based logic. No real fraud detection.

**CNP Fraud (Forter, Riskified, Signifyd, Accertify):**
- What they do: Checkout fraud prevention, identity verification, chargeback guarantees
- What they miss: Pre-transaction focus. Returns/claims capabilities are bolted on, rules-based, and lack in-store visibility. A customer blocked online can return in-store with no warning.

**Engage's structural advantage:** Appriss' Data Intelligence layer connects returns, claims, appeasements, and repeat behavior in a single platform. Competitors typically address one category. Engage connects the full picture informed by data visibility that no other provider can match.

### 3c. The AI That Powers Engage

Three complementary layers:

**Layer 1: Data Intelligence (The Foundation)** — Identity resolution, consortium intelligence, and data normalization. Without this layer (completely unique to Appriss), every AI decision is built on partial, fragmented data.

**Layer 2: Decision Intelligence (The Engine)** — ML models trained on 20+ years of transaction data, making real-time approve/warn/decline decisions in under one second. Wardrobing detection, tender laundering, discount fraud, and outlier identification.

**Layer 3: Workflow Intelligence (The Accelerator)** — Generative and agentic AI that interprets data, surfaces insights, and takes actions. Helps uncover where, when, why, and how returns are impacting your business so you can protect more revenue.

**Note: When to Go Deep on AI:** For most audiences, the differentiation narrative in Section 3a (Data Gap, AI Gap, Maintenance Gap) is sufficient. The three-layer AI architecture is most useful when presenting to technical buyers (CTO/IT) or when Appriss is specifically being evaluated as part of a retailer's AI strategy. Lead with outcomes, not architecture.

## 6. Audience Track: The Current Engage Customer

**Audience:** Existing Engage customers who are currently only actioning on non-receipted returns and haven't activated Engage for receipted return types.

### The Challenge

Many Engage customers deployed the platform initially to address non-receipted returns — the return type with the most obvious fraud signal. But this creates a significant blind spot. Non-receipted returns represent a fraction of total return volume. Most returns — and the majority of behavioral abuse — happens on receipted transactions.

**The Uncomfortable Truth:** Retailers running Engage only on non-receipted returns are only leveraging a fraction of the behavioral intelligence they're paying for. The $86B abuse category — bracketing, wardrobing, serial returning — almost always involves receipted transactions. Having a receipt doesn't prove legitimacy; it only proves someone learned your policy rules well enough to potentially abuse them. The omnichannel gap works against you.

### Why Receipted Activation Matters

1. **Receipted Returns Are Where the Volume — and the Abuse — Live.** Small percentage improvements across receipted volume deliver outsized financial impact.

2. **The Behavioral Middle Hides in Receipted Returns.** Serial returners, wardrobers, and policy exploiters almost always have receipts. They're not doing anything that looks like traditional fraud. Engage's behavioral intelligence identifies these patterns; rules-based systems don't.

3. **Omnichannel Coverage Requires Full Return Type Coverage.** Activating all return types allows for true cross-channel consistency and visibility.

4. **The AI Models Improve with More Data.** By excluding receipted returns, retailers miss out on potential value and open themselves up to more risk. Activating receipted returns strengthens the accuracy of every decision the system makes — including non-receipted decisions.

### The Activation Narrative

Frame the conversation around three themes:

- **Completeness:** "You're already seeing value on non-receipted returns. Activating receipted returns considers the full behavioral picture, drives consistency, and makes your non-receipted decisions more accurate, too."
- **FA mode is your proof mechanism:** "Let's run Engage on receipted returns in Forced Approval mode across your full fleet. The model evaluates every transaction and shows you exactly what it would have actioned on — without impacting a single customer. Your own data builds the business case for what you're losing every day you don't activate." (Note: piloting in a subset of stores should only be offered as a last resort if FA data and ROI modeling haven't moved the champion. It's not the default position.)
- **The cost of inaction:** "Every month without receipted returns activated is a month of behavioral abuse that goes undetected and unaddressed. The loss is happening whether or not you're measuring it. Let's quantify what that looks like with your data."

### Overcoming the "Stuck in FA" Problem

Some customers run receipted returns in FA mode for months and still hesitate to activate, even with strong ROI data. This is one of the most common expansion blockers. Approaches:

- **Social proof and FOMO:** Leading retailers across every category are already actioning on receipted returns. Customers who don't are falling behind their peers. Frame it as: "Your competitors are already using behavioral intelligence on all return types. Every month you stay in observation mode is a month they're pulling ahead."
- **Cultural shift:** Help the customer reframe the assumption that "receipted = safe." A receipt doesn't prove legitimacy — it proves the customer learned your policy. The $86B abuse category lives primarily in receipted transactions. Wardrobing, bracketing, serial returning — these all involve receipts.
- **Cost-of-inaction math:** Take the FA data and calculate the cumulative loss during every month of FA. "Based on your data, you've left $X on the table during the 4 months in FA mode. That number grows every month."
- **The accuracy story:** Less than 0.001% of Engage decisions receive a dispute or customer call. When Engage does act, it's highly precise. This isn't a blunt instrument — it's surgical.

**Note: If Fraudsters Are Using AI, Can You Keep Up Without It?**
Receipts don't equal safe. Fraudsters are increasingly using AI and social engineering to manipulate the returns process. If they're using technology to exploit your policies, the question becomes: can you keep up without also using AI? Powerful reframe for LP leaders who still see receipted returns as "low risk."
