## 3. The Returns Abuse Conversation Framework

Defining "abuse" is the top challenge we encounter. Every retailer defines it differently. Don't impose a definition — rather, facilitate a conversation that uncovers theirs and shows how Engage calibrates to match.

**Step 1: Uncover Their Definition**
- "When you think about return abuse, what does that look like for your business?"
- "Is it specific behaviors — wardrobing, bracketing, serial returns — or more of a volume/pattern issue?"
- "How do your store associates and operations teams identify it today? Is it consistent across locations/geos?"

**Step 2: Quantify the Impact**
- "Do you have a sense of what return abuse costs you annually — in dollars, margin, or operational overhead?"
- "What percentage of your returns would you estimate are problematic vs. legitimate?"

**Step 3: Explore Their Tolerance**
- "If you could draw a line between 'this is fine' and 'this needs to stop,' where would that line be?"
- "How aggressive do you want to be? Comfortable with denials, or start with warnings only?"

**Step 4: Bridge to Engage**
- **Reframe:** "We don't define abuse for you — we help you enforce your definition consistently, at scale, across every channel."
- **Model:** "The model evaluates individual behavior patterns, not single transactions. It identifies the small percentage of consumers whose return behavior is outside normal bounds — this can be customized based on your desired thresholds and refined over time."
- **CX bridge:** "The vast majority of your customers will not experience a massive change. And 90% of warned consumers continue buying."

**Key Reframe:** We help address the behaviors that cut into profitability — we don't punish customers. The model identifies patterns, not people. And 90% of consumers who receive a warning continue buying.

## 4. Persona Playbooks

### Loss Prevention VP / SVP
*Owns shrink, fraud, and safety strategy. Reports results to C-suite.*

**Pain Points:** Rising return fraud with limited cross-channel visibility; pressure to prove ROI on LP investments while total loss climbs; disconnected data between in-store, online, and call center.

**Value Narrative:** Engage connects the dots across every return touchpoint — giving you one real-time view of customer behavior. Instead of reacting after the fact, you're making accurate decisions at the point of return, with data to show the C-suite exactly what you're protecting.

**Discovery Starters:**
- "How are you measuring the impact of returns on total loss today?"
- "Do you have visibility into return behavior across all channels, or is that siloed?"
- "What does your current decisioning process look like at the point of return?"

**Objection Handling:**
- *"We only want receipted returns — non-receipted is too risky."* → Deploy in Forced Approval mode across all return types and let the data speak. With Engage deployed across all return types, insult rates stay low and 90% of warned customers buy again.
- *"We've tried returns authorization before and associates override it."* → That's usually a training and workflow problem. Engage minimizes associate friction — the system makes the decision. Override rates are trackable and typically drop significantly within 30 days.
- *"Our shrink number is more important than returns right now."* → Returns and shrink are very closely connected. Fraudulent returns are a major contributor to shrink. Engage makes that impact both visible and actionable.

### Loss Prevention Manager
*Day-to-day investigator. Manages cases, alerts, and store team coordination.*

**Pain Points:** Alert overload with too many false positives; manual digging through disconnected systems to build cases; limited headcount to focus on investigations that move the needle.

**Value Narrative:** Engage surfaces the high-risk patterns automatically — serial abusers, organized schemes, policy exploiters across all channels — so your team spends time on warranted investigations, not data wrangling.

**Discovery Starters:**
- "How much time does your team spend manually researching return patterns?"
- "When a return gets flagged, how do you determine if it's worth investigating?"
- "Do you have a way to see if a customer is returning across multiple channels?"

**Objection Handling:**
- *"We don't have bandwidth to manage another tool."* → Engage actually reduces your workload — it replaces the manual digging needed to identify fraud and abuse. The system surfaces high-value patterns so your team can focus on actioning.
- *"Store teams won't enforce non-receipted declines — they're afraid of confrontation."* → The warning tier is designed for exactly this. It gives associates a system-backed message that redirects behavior without conflict.

### Finance Leader (CFO / VP Finance)
*Focused on margins, EBITDA, and justifying investments with clear ROI.*

**Pain Points:** Returns hit margins hard, and the true cost (labor, shipping, write-downs) is hard to quantify; competing capital allocation — needs a clear, defensible business case.

**Value Narrative:** Every return costs more than just the refund — processing costs alone can be up to 30% of the item's value. Engage quantifies and reduces that exposure by making smarter decisions at every return touchpoint. The payback is fast and measurable.

**Discovery Starters:**
- "How are you currently quantifying the total cost of returns to your P&L?"
- "What would it mean to your margins if you could reduce return fraud by even 8-10%? What about reducing your return rate overall?"
- "What does the approval process look like for a new loss-related investment?"

**Objection Handling:**
- *"The ROI sounds good but I need to see it against our numbers."* → That's exactly the right ask. We'll run an ROI analysis using your actual return data — this functions as a collaborative exercise, not a black box.
- *"We can't justify the spend on a small pilot."* → The Forced Approval period is how you build the business case. We start with FA mode across your fleet, model the impact and ROI with your own data, and that becomes the investment thesis for activation.

### Operations Leader (Store / Retail Ops)
*Manages processes, training, and consistency across locations with high turnover.*

**Pain Points:** Returns create friction at the register — associates don't know what to do with edge cases; high turnover means constant retraining on returns policies; inconsistent enforcement across locations.

**Value Narrative:** Engage takes judgment calls off your associates' plate. The system makes the decision in real time, consistently, across every store. Less conflict, less training burden, more consistent enforcement.

**Discovery Starters:**
- "How do your store associates handle returns decisions today — manual or system-guided?"
- "What's your biggest pain point with returns at the store level?"
- "How long does it take to train a new associate on your returns process?"

**Objection Handling:**

### Ecommerce / Digital Leader
*Owns online profitability. Manages digital return/claim rates while protecting CX.*

**Pain Points:** High online return rates and growing claims erode digital margin; no visibility into whether online returners are also abusing in-store or via the customer service center.

**Value Narrative:** Someone gaming your online return or claims process who is also returning in-store shows up as one risk profile, not two separate events. You protect digital margins without creating friction for legitimate customers.

## 5. Objection Handling by Theme

### Theme 1: Customer Experience Impact
*Underlying fear: "If we start denying returns, we'll lose good customers."*

**Reframe:** Engage protects ~99% of customers by only denying the true examples of fraud and abuse. At the same time, warnings help change behavior without losing the customer. The alternative is blanket policy tightening that risks being too strict and punishing your best customers.

**Proof Points:** 90% of warned consumers continue to buy; 99%+ of consumers are never impacted; less than 0.001% of Engage decisions receive a dispute or customer call. (Note: Appriss does not monitor NPS on behalf of customers. The dispute rate is the accuracy proof point — use it instead of NPS claims.)

### Theme 2: ROI Credibility
*Underlying fear: "These numbers feel too good to be true."*

**Reframe:** Separate the stories. Return rate reduction (8-12% receipted) is the Engage metric. Shrink reduction is a Secure metric. Offer to model against their own data pre-sale and during FA mode and encourage them to keep in mind that we feel so confident in our value that we will guarantee ROI as long as certain criteria are met in implementation.

**Proof Points:** 8-12% return rate decrease on receipted; 3:1+ ROI at full deployment; FA mode = zero-risk evaluation against their own data.

### Theme 3: Policy Logic
*Underlying fear: "We can solve this with better policies. We don't need a model."*

**Reframe:** Policies are binary; the model is behavioral. Rules catch known patterns; the model catches evolving cross-retailer behavior. And policies rely on consistent associate enforcement — the #1 operational gap retailers cite.

**Proof Points:** Appriss retailer consortium provides data no single retailer can replicate; model adapts to evolving patterns without manual rule updates; associates get clear decision outcomes instead of relying on judgment calls.

## 6. Competitive Landscape

Position Engage based on channel and incumbent. The overarching narrative: Engage is the unified decision engine that works across all return channels and retailer tech stacks.

**Critical approach note:** When discovery reveals a prospect is already using a competitor for online/digital returns, do NOT lead by attacking the competitor. Instead, lead with curiosity — ask how the existing solution is working for them. Then lean into Appriss' in-store strength (the competitor's blind spot) and position Engage as complementary. Only differentiate on intelligence claims if the customer raises it.

### In-Store: Engage vs. Narvar vs. Homegrown

**Narvar:** Offers free in-store returns to get in the door, but lacks the data intelligence needed to make decisions based on the full picture of customer behavior (limited to online behavior data). Their real value prop is workflow and customer communication — not decisioning. Engage is the intelligence layer. **Key talking point: Narvar, Loop, and parcelLab don't touch in-store transactions. In-store is Appriss' bread and butter — and for most retailers, especially when talking to LP, it's the bigger area of pain and opportunity.**

**Homegrown:** Rules-based policies enforced inconsistently by associates. Engage replaces guesswork with AI-driven, consistent decisioning across all store locations.

### Online: Engage vs. Forter / Riskified / Signifyd vs. Narvar / parcelLab / Loop

**CNP fraud focus (Forter/Riskified/Signifyd):** Solve payment fraud at purchase. Engage solves return abuse at the return. Completely different vector. Complementary tools. If they extend into returns: their models are built on purchase fraud, not return behavior. Engage has 20+ years of return-specific behavioral data.

**Narvar / parcelLab / Loop:** Their real value prop is an easy returns workflow and clear customer communication — not decisioning. They claim returns intelligence/fraud authorization capabilities, but their data visibility is limited to online behavior data. If the customer raises this: differentiate on consortium data depth, cross-channel visibility, and 20+ years of behavioral modeling maturity. But lead with in-store opportunity, not with attacking the competitor's digital capabilities.

**The Decision Engine Positioning:** Engage sits between any return portal — Optoro, Narvar, ParcelLab, or homegrown — and provides accurate, consistent authorization decisions across every channel. We're not replacing your workflow; we're making it smarter.

## 8. Adoption & Optimization Guide (Maturity Framework)

### The Returns Maturity Journey

**Core Narrative:** Moving through the returns maturity journey is about building trust, proving value, and showing that balance is achievable. Every Engage customer follows a maturity path — from Forced Approval through confident expansion to strategic transformation. The best outcomes come when retailers move deliberately, but quickly through each stage.

### Three Pillars of the Returns Maturity Journey

At each stage, retailers balance three interconnected pillars:

| Fraud & Abuse Reduction | Customer Experience | Operational Efficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Return rate reduction, fraud identification, abuse pattern detection, habitual returner surfacing | Low insult rate, reward good customers, configurable thresholds tied to risk tolerance, 90%+ warned customers buy again | Override rate reduction, call rate management, associate consistency, process standardization, personalized policies at scale |

### The Approve / Warn / Deny Framework

At the heart of Engage is a three-tier decision model that evolves as the retailer matures. In early stages, the focus is on learning patterns in FA (forced approval) mode. As confidence builds, retailers shift from observation to action — moving from Forced Approval to active decisioning (warnings and denials), then expanding to additional store groups and/or channels.

**The key insight: this is not a binary approve/deny system. The "Warn" tier is where the magic happens — it changes behavior without losing the customer.**

**Proof Point:** 90% of warned customers buy again and return rates drop from 13.7% to 12% with Appriss (portfolio avg.; individual results vary).

### CRAWL — Observe & Validate (Weeks 1-3)

FA mode across fleet → validate data → activate

**What This Stage Looks Like:** The retailer has deployed Engage in Forced Approval (FA) mode across their full fleet. The model is running and generating recommendations but not impacting customers. Goal: validate data quality, establish baselines, and activate as quickly as possible — ideally within 2 weeks, 3 weeks maximum.

**Health Signals:**
- Healthy: Data flowing with low error rate; key contacts identified and responsive; activation timeline agreed (target: 2 weeks); internal champion reviewing FA reports
- At Risk: Data gaps or integration issues causing delays; single contact, no cross-functional visibility; FA running 3+ weeks with no activation date; no one looking at the data / low engagement

**How to Move Them Forward:**
→ Schedule a calibration review within the first week to walk through FA results
→ Show them what the model would have done — quantify the "value they're leaving on the table" every week they stay in FA
→ Propose a time-bound activation: "Let's go live by [date] and review results at 30 days"
→ If stalled, identify the blocker. It's usually not data confidence — it's sponsor buy-in, internal change management, or resource constraints. Re-engage the right person.
→ **Lean into internal resources:** Loop in Eric Miller and the SE team to pull real examples of fraud and abuse from the customer's own FA data. Showing the retailer specific anecdotes of behaviors Engage flagged — real transactions, real patterns — is often what gets a hesitant champion to act.

**When They're Stuck:** The most common Crawl stall is a retailer who stays in FA mode indefinitely because no one is championing activation. The fix is finding or re-engaging the right sponsor. Use benchmarking: "Here's where you sit vs. peers who have activated — this is the gap." Also quantify the cumulative cost of inaction: "Based on your FA data, you've left $X on the table during the N weeks in FA mode."

**Urgency Principle:** The longer a retailer sits in FA mode, the harder it is to prove value. The ideal FA period is 2 weeks — enough to validate data quality and build a baseline, not enough to become a comfort zone. 3 weeks is the max.

**Important FA mode limitation:** In FA mode, you cannot observe operational metrics like call rates, override rates, or insult rates — because Engage isn't actually actioning on returns yet. FA shows what the model *would have done*. Use FA data to project ROI and quantify the gap, but don't reference metrics that only exist once active decisioning is live.

### WALK — Activate & Expand (Months 1-3)

Active decisioning → channel expansion → calibration

**What This Stage Looks Like:** The retailer has activated Engage and is seeing real-time decisions impacting customers. Override rates are declining, insult rates are being monitored and within expected range, and the internal narrative is shifting from "are we sure?" to "here's what we're seeing." Focus: optimizing thresholds and expanding to additional channels.

**Health Signals:**
- Healthy: Active decisioning live, approve/warn/deny ratios healthy; override rates declining (<25% and trending down); return rate reduction being monitored; channel expansion conversation happening
- At Risk: Activation delayed or rolled back; override rates increasing after 30 days with no plan for associate coaching; no measurable movement after 60-90 days; retailer considers themselves "done" after initial activation

**How to Move Them Forward:**
→ Conduct a formal calibration review — are thresholds aligned with their tolerance?
→ Introduce benchmarking: show where their action rates sit vs. peer group
→ Frame the expansion conversation: "You're seeing X impact on in-store — here's what online would add"
→ If override rates are high: it's almost always a training issue, not a threshold issue. Work with Store Ops to improve associate confidence and consistency.

**When They're Stuck:** Walk stalls come from two places: (1) thresholds too conservative — a calibration review showing what "more aggressive" looks like in concrete terms is the answer; or (2) they see value but lack momentum to expand — use the SBR to tell the expansion story: benchmark their coverage vs. peers, quantify the gap, and make the next step feel low-risk.

### RUN — Optimize & Scale (Months 3-6)

Full omnichannel → optimized thresholds → strategic value

**What This Stage Looks Like:** The retailer is running Engage across multiple channels with optimized thresholds and measurable ROI. The conversation shifts from "is this working?" to "how do we get more out of this?" This is where Engage becomes a strategic tool, not just an operational one.

**Health Signals:**
- Healthy: Omnichannel active (in-store + online); ROI exceeding 3:1, Finance can stand behind it; multiple stakeholders consuming Engage insights; business reviews are strategy sessions, not justification
- At Risk: Stuck in single channel / online stalled; ROI declining or value being questioned; only LP engaged, no cross-functional visibility; reviews feel defensive, sponsor losing interest or turnover introducing risk

**How to Move Them Forward:**
→ Build a formal ROI case for the next SBR with their own data
→ Introduce the Ultimate tier: add-on capabilities such as Order Protection and ID Verification
→ Identify cross-sell potential: are they a Secure candidate? Incident?
→ Get Engage data in front of broader stakeholders — Finance for ROI, Ecom for digital strategy

**When They're Stuck:** Run stalls are usually about internal visibility more than product issues. The retailer is getting value but no one above the LP director knows. The fix is arming your champion with an internal story: a 1-page executive summary, an ROI slide for their quarterly review, benchmarking data they can share upward.

### BEST-IN-CLASS — Transform & Lead (6-8 months)

Strategic platform → personalized policies → enterprise value

**What This Stage Looks Like:** The retailer has fully integrated Engage into their returns strategy. They're on the Ultimate/highest level package tier and getting the most out of personalized policies and cross-channel intelligence. These are often your reference accounts and Appriss advocates.

**Health Signals:**
- Healthy: Premium tier capabilities active; Engage data informing broader business decisions, Appriss viewed as strategic partner; active reference/advocate; cross-product engagement (Secure, Incident)
- At Risk: Contract renewal approaching without expansion conversation; key champion departing, no succession plan; competitor making aggressive displacement play; retailer in maintenance mode, relationship drifting

**How to Move Them Forward:**
→ Deepen the partnership: solicit executive sponsorship, engage in joint roadmap sessions, explore advisory board and co-marketing opps
→ Secure reference commitments: case studies, analyst briefings, prospect calls
→ Explore cross-product expansion: Secure for shrink, Incident for ORC, new beta capabilities
→ Ensure renewal includes expansion and pricing appropriate to value delivered

**When They're Stuck:** Best-in-Class accounts don't stall — they drift. The risk is complacency: maintenance-mode relationship where a competitor or internal initiative displaces Engage. The counter is proactive engagement: strategic reviews (not just SBRs), exec-to-exec relationship building, and ensuring Engage is deeply embedded in operational workflow so that switching costs are high.

## 9. Benchmarking & Expansion Readiness

### Key Benchmarking Dimensions

| Dimension | What to Compare | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Action Rates (Receipted) | % of receipted returns receiving warn/deny vs. approve | Shows whether the retailer is too conservative or aggressive relative to peers |
| Action Rates (Non-Receipted) | % of non-receipted returns actioned | Non-receipted is where the majority of customers get "stuck" — often room to optimize and also expand into receipted |
| Override Rates | % of model recommendations overridden by associates | High override rates = training gaps, threshold misalignment, or lack of confidence |
| Return Rate Change | Pre vs. post-activation return rate | The core Engage value metric. Full return rate assessment requires a year for seasonality; monitor trends early |
| Channel Coverage | In-store only vs. omnichannel | Single-channel retailers are leaving value on the table |
| Warn-to-Purchase Rate | % of warned consumers who make a subsequent purchase | The CX proof point — warnings change behavior, not lose the customer |

**Benchmarking Best Practice:** Frame it as opportunity, not criticism. "Here's where you sit. Here's where your peers sit. Here's the gap." Let the retailer draw the conclusion.

### Expansion Readiness Signals
- Customer asking "what else can we do?" — frame the next tier or channel
- Strong ROI documented — Finance engaged and satisfied; use as foundation for expansion
- Champion promoting internally — arm them with materials; get in front of new buyers
- Approaching renewal — natural moment to bundle expansion with renewal
- New leadership or org change — new champion wants to make their mark; expand scope

### Cross-Sell Quick Reference
- **Engage → Secure:** Customer has returns optimization but not exception-based reporting (EBR) for broader shrink. Signal: LP team using Engage data for investigations and wanting more.
- **Secure → Engage:** Customer has EBR for shrink but not managing returns with AI. Signal: returns come up in SBRs as a pain point.
- **Engage → Incident:** Customer using Engage data to identify organized return schemes and needs case management. Signal: LP team manually tracking repeat offenders.

**CSM comfort with cross-sell:** You're not selling — you're identifying a gap in their coverage. Position it as: "I've noticed X in your data. There's a tool that addresses this — want me to set up a conversation with your AE?"

## 10. Technical Readiness Overview

### What Data Does Engage Need?

| Data Category | Priority | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| POS Returns & Transactions | Required | The foundation. Transaction ID, date, location, amount, and type. 12 months of history recommended. |
| Customer Identity | Required | Powers identity linking. Name, email, phone, loyalty ID, and address signals get consolidated into a single entity. |
| Item Detail | Required | SKU, product description, quantity, price, discount. Enables item-level risk scoring. |
| Tender / Payment | High | Payment tokens, card type, masked card number. Improve cross-channel identity linking. |
| Ecom Returns & Claims | High | Online order ID, claim type (appeasement, reshipment, INR), refund channel. Unlocks omnichannel visibility. |
| Call Center / CS Data | Medium | CS transaction records. Catches customers who shift channels to avoid detection. |

### Implementation Timeline

A typical Engage deployment is ~12-16 weeks from kickoff to live decisioning. Workstreams run concurrently.

| Phase | Timing | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| Prerequisites | Pre-kickoff | Retailer provides POS/ecom production data, store reference file, and establishes data delivery method. |
| Data Integration & Mapping | Weeks 1-7 | Appriss maps retailer data, configures consumer identity linking, and builds data views. |
| POS Integration & Config | Weeks 2-6 | Retailer builds the API call into their POS (standard REST API). Appriss provides documentation and test cases. |
| Model Development | Weeks 5-9 | Appriss trains the behavioral model on ~4 months of data. Model review session with retailer. |
| Testing & Go-Live | Weeks 8-12+ | POS integration certification. Internal QA. User training and rollout to stores. |

**What Drives Timeline Variability:** The #1 factor is speed of data delivery. If data is ready at contract signature, the clock starts immediately. Other factors: POS system complexity, number of channels in scope, security reviews, and IT freeze windows.

**Talk track:** "From kickoff to live decisioning in pilot stores, plan for about 12-16 weeks. Your team's biggest lift is upfront — getting data flowing and building the POS integration. Once that's done, we carry the load on model development, and IT involvement drops to near-zero after go-live."

### Common Technical Questions

**Q: How much IT time does this require?** Typically 40-80 hours total across the implementation. Heaviest in weeks 1-6. Post go-live, IT involvement is minimal — just monitoring data feeds.

**Q: What POS systems do you integrate with?** All major platforms via standard REST API. Sub-second response time (1/5th of a second). Our implementation team handles the specifics.

**Q: What if our API goes down?** Configurable failover — default is to approve the return so there is no disruption to customers. Uptime target >99.5%.

**Q: Can we pilot before a full rollout?** That's the standard approach. We deploy in Forced Approval mode, validate the model, then activate decisioning and measure results.

**Q: Do we need new infrastructure?** No. Cloud-hosted, SOC 2 Type II compliant, standard REST API. No proprietary hardware or protocols. Supports vendor security reviews.

**Q: Can our business rules be incorporated?** Yes, though we recommend keeping static rules minimal — the behavioral model performs best with flexibility. Specific item or customer-tier exceptions can be configured.
