# Appriss Retail Tone of Voice

## The Core Vibe

Authoritative and accessible. Conversational and confident. Noticeably more casual than buttoned-up. Practical, not promotional.

Think: a seasoned retail expert who's easy to talk to—and clearly knows their stuff.

---

## Voice Principles

### Sound Human, Not Corporate
- Write like a smart colleague explaining something, not a press release
- Use contractions naturally (we're, don't, it's)
- Keep sentences short to medium—mix in longer ones where they flow naturally, but don't overexplain
- Plain English first; industry terms only when necessary and used correctly
- Write the way one senior leader would speak to another: clear, confident, peer-level

### Be Confident Without Being Pushy
- Guide rather than declare
- Earn trust through insight, not volume
- Calm, steady energy—no hype, no theatrics, no sales pressure
- Let the work speak; don't oversell it

### Stay Warm, Not Stiff
- Light personality is welcome
- Warmth and approachability are assets
- Never sarcastic, but never sterile either

### Frame Through Business Impact
- Lead with measurable outcomes: margin, efficiency, risk, operational impact
- Avoid vague claims—be specific when possible
- Ground insights in real patterns and use cases
- Frame around what the reader cares about, not what we want to say about ourselves

## Outbound Email Guidelines

These apply specifically to prospecting and outreach emails.

### Do's
- **Lead with their world, not ours.** Open with something relevant to the recipient — their role, a challenge common to their industry, a recent company development, or content they've posted. One specific, authentic detail beats generic flattery every time.
- **Get to value fast.** Don't warm up. The first sentence should signal why this is worth reading.
- **Name-drop peers.** Include a natural reference to companies we work with: *"We help companies like [X, Y, Z]…"*
- **One CTA. Keep it low-friction.** Examples that work: *"Worth a quick look?"* or *"Open to a short chat?"* — not *"Book a 30-minute discovery call."*
- **Vary your sentence structure.** Mix short punchy sentences with longer ones. Avoid repeating the same structure back-to-back.
- **Max one emoji per email.** And only if it fits naturally.

### Don'ts
- **Don't open with "I."** The email should be about them, not you.
- **Don't use these openers — ever:**
  - "I hope this email finds you well…"
  - "I noticed…"
  - "I'll keep this brief…"
  - "I see your experience…"
- **Don't use these phrases — ever:**
  - "Circling back…"
  - "Just following up on my previous email…"
  - "Book a 30-minute call…"
  - "Don't miss this opportunity."
- **Don't repeat the prospect's name** more than once (avoid: "Hi Sarah… Sarah, I wanted to…").
- **Don't use more than one exclamation point** in an email — ideally zero.
- **Don't create walls of text.** Short paragraphs, white space, scannable.
- **Don't use repetitive sentence structures or transition words.** Avoid leaning on "Additionally," "Furthermore," "In conclusion," or restating the same point in different words. Every sentence should add something new.
- **Don't crowd sentences** with too many examples or parenthetical lists. One strong example beats three weak ones.
- **Don't use acronyms** unless you've defined them earlier in the email.
- **No over-personal flattery.** Specific and genuine beats gushing.

## What Good Looks Like

**Newsletter intro (The Takeback):**
> "Welcome to the first edition of The Takeback. Each issue, we're sharing the strategies, data, and insights from retail leaders who can help you understand the core components of retail loss—and start to take back what's been slipping away."

**LinkedIn post (NRF recap):**
> "NRF isn't just about what's said on stage. It's about what happens around the conversations. This year, Appriss Retail showed up the way we believe retail works best: human, collaborative, and a little unexpected."

**Explaining a concept (from The Takeback):**
> "Revenue protection works like a firewall. If you turn it off, the attacks get through. The complete picture is that loss and returns affect top-line sales, margins, and cash flow simultaneously."

**Quoting a customer/expert:**
> "CFOs are interested in anything that's gonna drive top-line sales and expand margins. They're also interested in anything that doesn't require additional headcount or processes."

## Hard No's—Never Use These

| Banned Words/Phrases | Why |
|---------------------|-----|
| Synergy | Corporate cliché |
| Leverage (unless literal) | Overused buzzword |
| Revolutionize | Hype |
| Cutting-edge | Cliché |
| Unprecedented | Overused |
| Best-in-class | Empty superlative |
| Game-changer | Hype word |
| Disrupt / Disruptive | Tech buzzword |
| Holistic | Vague and overused |
| Robust | Meaningless filler |
| Journey | Corporate-speak |
| Value prop | Jargon |
| "In today's world" | Lazy opener |
| Gen Z slang | Off-brand |
| Inspirational/motivational tone | We inform, not inspire |

## Channel-Specific Notes

These guidelines apply across all channels. The tone stays consistent whether you're writing:

- **Outbound emails** — Recipient-first. One clear value signal, one CTA, no filler openers. See the outbound email section above for specifics.
- **LinkedIn posts** — Conversational and human. It's okay to be reflective or share a point of view. Avoid hashtag spam.
- **Website/landing pages** — Clear and scannable. Lead with what matters to the reader, not what we want to say about ourselves.
- **Newsletters** — Warm and collegial. Write like you're catching up a peer on what they need to know.

---

## The Litmus Test

Before publishing, ask:

1. Would a retail expert actually talk this way?
2. Does it sound like a real person or a corporate deck?
3. Is there any hype or empty language that could be cut?
4. Does it lead with impact or features?
5. Would you want to read this?
