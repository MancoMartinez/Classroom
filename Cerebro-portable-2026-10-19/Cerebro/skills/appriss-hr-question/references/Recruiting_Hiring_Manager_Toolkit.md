## 1. Hiring & Job Posting Request

Before any role can be posted or filled, it needs to go through the approval process below. This applies to all new hires, backfills, and contractor conversions.

### Step 1: Identify the Need

- Identify the staffing need early. Submit your request ahead of any immediate demand.
- Think through: Is this a new role or a backfill? Full-time employee or contractor? What level and title?
- Have a comp range in mind before submitting. People Ops can help with benchmarking if needed.

### Step 2: Submit the Hiring Request

Complete the **Jira Hiring Request Form** with all fields accurately. This form drives the entire downstream process. Incomplete submissions will be returned.

**Required fields include:** Role title, department, reporting manager, employment type (FTE/contractor), expected start date, justification for the hire, and proposed compensation range.

### Step 3: Approval Routing

Once submitted, the request automatically routes to all required approvers in parallel. All parties must approve before the role can be posted.

| Approver | What They Review | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring Manager's ELT | Business justification and headcount need | Parallel — all must approve simultaneously |
| CFO (Andrew Weeks) | Budget and financial impact | Parallel — all must approve simultaneously |
| People Ops | Role structure, title, comp alignment | Parallel — all must approve simultaneously |

> **IMPORTANT:** ELT members route directly to CFO and People Ops. If you are ELT, your request does not need a separate ELT approval — it goes straight to CFO and People Ops.

### Step 4: Approved or Denied

1. **Approved:** If approved, you will receive a job posting form.
2. **Declined:** CFO/ELT will add comments and return it to you for edits. Revise and resubmit.

### Step 5: Job Posting Request

If the hire is approved, you will receive a Job Posting Form to complete.

**It is attached to the previously submitted Hiring Request Form.** Fill it out promptly — posting creation takes 1–2 business days after People Ops receives it.

The information contained in the Job Posting Request Form will be relied on for creating the job requisition posting. Please ensure all information submitted is accurate.

> **Required fields include:** Pipeline plan, knockout questions, short answer questions, who will need access for reviewing applications.

### Step 6: Job Posting Confirmation

The Hiring Manager will be notified once the job posting has been created and given access to a preview. The Hiring Manager should ensure all details are correct before approving the job posting.

### Job Posting Details

Once approved, jobs are posted for **30 days** with no evergreen postings. They auto-distribute to:

- LinkedIn, Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Monster
- talent.com, MyJobHelper, Adzuna, Jora, Jooble
- Appriss Retail career page

**Specialty boards:** If a role needs posting on a niche job board (e.g., engineering-specific, legal, etc.), contact People Ops. Standard boards are automated.

## 2. Rippling ATS: Navigation Guide

Rippling Recruiting (RR) is our Applicant Tracking System (ATS). It manages job requisition, candidate pipelines, applicant email templates, and offer workflows.

> **Note:** Rippling Recruiting is NOT integrated with MS Suite, so you will not be able to schedule interviews directly from RR. You will have to do this externally. Reach out to People Ops if you need help.

### Logging In and Finding Your Requisition

1. **Go to:** https://app.rippling.com/dashboard
2. **Click:** The Recruiting widget on your homepage dashboard.
3. **Click:** Job Requisitions tab at the top.
4. **Click:** The applicable job requisition to open it.

### Reviewing Candidates

Once inside a requisition, you will see all candidates at every pipeline stage. There are two ways to review them:

#### Option A: Start Application Review (linear)

Click **"Start application review"** at the top of the candidate list. This takes you through each candidate one at a time in a sequential queue. You can move forward, reject, or skip.

#### Option B: Select a Candidate in the Queue (recommended)

Click directly on a candidate's name. This opens the full profile and gives you more flexibility and detail than the linear view.

From either view you can:

- Download or view the resume and LinkedIn profile
- View answers to any screening questions on the requisition
- Move the candidate forward or reject them
- Add shared comments (visible to the team) or private notes (visible only to you)
- Tag someone via @mention to notify them about a candidate

### Candidate Pipeline Stages

Candidates move through the stages below. Order can be modified/adjusted to fit your team's needs.

| # | Stage | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Application Review | Hiring Manager / Christine Kull |
| 2 | Screen with People Ops | People Ops / Christine Kull |
| 3 | Interview with Team | Hiring Manager |
| 4 | Interview with Hiring Manager | Hiring Manager |
| 5 | Skills Assessment | Hiring Manager |
| 6 | Final Interview | Hiring Manager / ELT |
| 7 | Offer / Offer Accepted | People Ops + HM |

> **Christine Kull Team / People Ops screens:** Send no more than 10 candidates at a time for screening. Wait for that batch to complete before sending more.

### Dispositions: Moving Candidates and Rejecting

Use the action buttons at the top right of any candidate profile to disposition them.

- **Move Forward:** Advances the candidate one step in the pipeline.
- **Reject:** Removes the candidate. Select a rejection reason from the dropdown and send a templated rejection email.
- **Three-dot menu (...):** Move to another stage (to skip steps), email candidate, copy profile link, add to another requisition, or edit candidate info.

> **IMPORTANT:** "Move Forward" only advances one step at a time. To skip stages, always use the three-dot menu > "Move to another stage."

### Entering Interview Feedback

Every interviewer should enter feedback in Rippling **immediately after each interview. Do not wait.**

1. **Navigate to the candidate:** Select the correct pipeline step for the stage you just completed.
2. **Click Add Feedback:** Located next to the relevant interview stage.
3. **Rate the candidate:** Choose Strong No, No, Yes, or Strong Yes. Add written notes.
4. **Submit Feedback:** Click Submit Feedback. Done.

> **IMPORTANT:** Do not discuss your assessment with other interviewers before submitting your independent feedback. This prevents anchoring bias.

**Best practices:** Review applicants within the 30-day posting window. Extend start date offers that are at least 2 weeks out from acceptance, on the 1st or 16th to align with payroll.

## 3. Interview Resources

Use these resources for every interview. Structured interviews reduce bias and produce better hiring decisions.

### Interview Structure – Summary

#### Before the Interview

- Review the candidate's resume in Rippling. Do not go in cold.
- Select 4–5 behavioral questions from the Behavioral Interview Questions section. Use the same questions for every candidate for the same role.
- Have the Candidate Evaluation Form & Process Guide ready to score as you go.
- Review the Lawful vs. Discriminatory Questions Guide (section below) if you are new to interviewing.

#### Step 1: Introduction (5 minutes)

- Introduce yourself and any panel members.
- Overview the interview format and timeline.
- Remind the candidate that Appriss Retail uses E-Verify for work authorization (US employees).
- Talk about our culture and what makes Appriss Retail unique.
- Describe the position and department structure.
- Ask if they have questions before you begin.

#### Step 2: Behavioral Questions (25–35 minutes)

- Ask your 4–5 pre-selected questions. Listen for STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- Take notes on specific examples. Probe vague answers: *"Can you walk me through a specific example?"*
- Score each category on the Evaluation Form as you go.
- Do not lead. Do not give away the right answer.

#### Step 3: Close (5 minutes)

- Ask if the candidate has questions.
- Reiterate next steps and timing.
- Thank them for their time.

#### After the Interview (5 minutes)

- Complete the Candidate Evaluation Form immediately. Do it while the conversation is fresh.
- Upload the completed form to the candidate's Rippling profile under Documents.
- Enter your rating in Rippling's feedback system (Strong No / No / Yes / Strong Yes).
- Communicate next steps to People Ops or the interview panel.

### Behavioral Interview Questions

Select 4–5 questions and use them consistently across all candidates for the same role.

These questions are designed around the **STAR method**. Listen for a specific **S**ituation, the **T**ask the person was responsible for, the **A**ction they took, and the **R**esult.

#### Problem Solving & Critical Thinking
- Tell me about a time you faced a complex problem. How did you approach it?
- Describe a situation where you had to think outside the box to solve a challenge.
- Tell me about a time you resisted the temptation to jump to a conclusion. What was the outcome?
- Describe a situation where a simple solution turned out to be the best one.

#### Leadership
- Give an example of a time you took the lead on a project without being asked.
- Tell me about a time when you inspired someone to take action. What did you do and what was the result?
- Describe a time when you had to lead a group of people who were resistant to change.

#### Communication & Interpersonal Skills
- Tell me about a time you had to convey difficult information to someone. How did you handle it?
- Tell me about a time when you had to win someone over with your words.
- Tell me about a time you had to adjust your communication style to leverage a relationship.
- Describe a time when reading between the lines in a conversation made all the difference.

#### Teamwork & Collaboration
- Give an example of a time you worked with a difficult teammate. How did you handle it?
- Tell me about a time you had to smooth over a disagreement between two coworkers.
- Give an example of a time when you had to compromise to move a team project forward.
- Have you ever had to navigate office politics to get something done? How did you handle it?

#### Conflict Resolution
- Describe a time you had a disagreement with a colleague. How did you resolve it?
- Have you ever played peacemaker between two people who just could not see eye to eye?
- Give an example of a time when you had to address a conflict with a supervisor or manager.

#### Customer Service & Client Management
- Give an example of a time you went above and beyond for a customer or client.
- Have you ever had a customer who was impossible to please? How did you turn it around?
- Have you ever had to say no to a customer while keeping them happy?

#### Time Management & Prioritization
- Imagine you are juggling five urgent tasks and your manager adds a sixth. What is your game plan?
- Describe a situation where you had to meet a tight deadline with limited resources.
- Walk me through a complicated project you planned. How did you define and measure success?

#### Ethics & Integrity
- Give an example of a time you witnessed unethical behavior at work. What did you do?
- Have you ever been in a situation where doing the right thing was not the easiest option?
- Recall a time where you made a major mistake. What did you do after? What changed?

#### Resilience & Handling Pressure
- Describe a situation where you had to work under significant stress. What did you do?
- Tell me about a time when everything went wrong at once. How did you keep going?

#### Handling Failure
- Have you ever had a big idea that completely flopped? What happened and what did you learn?
- Tell me about a time when failure ended up being the best thing that could have happened.
- Describe a time when you took a risk and it did not pay off. What would you do differently?

#### Flexibility & Adaptability
- Tell me about a time you had to adjust quickly to changes outside of your control.
- Give an example of a time when you had to learn something new in a short period.
- Have you ever had to embrace change overnight? How did you adjust?

#### Analytical Skills
- Tell me about a time you defined several solutions to a problem. How did you decide?
- Have you ever spotted a trend before anyone else? How did you act on it?
- Tell me about a time when you had to break down a mess of data to find the real story.

#### Career Goals & Motivation
- How do you personally measure success and happiness as it pertains to your job?
- Why do you want to work for Appriss Retail and what do you know about our company?
- Fast forward five years. What kind of impact do you want to have made?
- What have you done in the last year to improve or grow your professional worth?

#### Performance & Decision Making
- If we spoke to your previous supervisor, what would they say about your work performance?
- Tell me about a time when you outperformed even your own expectations.
- Give an example of a time you had to be quick in coming to an important decision.
- Have you ever had to make a tough call with no clear right answer? How did you decide?

#### Initiative & Passion
- Give me an example of a time you had to go above and beyond to get a job done.
- Describe a time when you turned a challenge into an opportunity without being prompted.
- Tell me one thing you are passionate about and how you demonstrate your passion.
- What is one work-related topic you could talk about for hours?

### Lawful vs. Discriminatory Interview Questions

This is a reference guide for formulating lawful pre-employment inquiries. When in doubt, do not ask. If a candidate volunteers protected information, do not write it down and do not factor it into your decision.

> **IMPORTANT:** This list is not exhaustive and does not reflect each state's anti-discrimination laws in full. Contact People Ops if you are unsure about a specific question.

| Topic | OK to Ask | Do NOT Ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Age** | Are you 18 years of age or older? Can you provide a work permit if under 18? | How old are you? What is your date of birth? May I see your birth certificate? |
| **Marital Status** | Do you have any commitments that might affect your attendance? Can you work the required shift? | Are you married? Do you have children? What is your spouse's name or income? |
| **Sex** | Only if there is a bona fide occupational qualification. | Are you pregnant? Are you planning to have children? Do you use birth control? |
| **Sexual Orientation** | None. | Any question about sexual or affectional preference. |
| **Medical / Disability** | Can you perform the essential functions of this role with or without reasonable accommodation? | Do you have disabilities? Have you filed workers' compensation? Are you an alcoholic? |
| **Race** | None. Race is never a bona fide occupational qualification. | What is your race? How tall/what color is your skin/hair/eyes? |
| **Religion** | None. You may state the work schedule; the applicant may comment on availability. | What religion are you? Which holidays will you be unavailable for? |
| **National Origin** | None. | Where were you born? What is your nationality or first language? |
| **Military** | Have you served in the US Armed Forces? (Only when directly relevant to the role.) | What type of discharge? Do you have military obligations? Have you served in a foreign military? |
| **Citizenship** | Are you a US citizen? Are you legally permitted to work in the US? | Are you naturalized or native-born? May I see your naturalization papers? |
| **Financial** | None. These questions discriminate against lower-income candidates. | Have you declared bankruptcy? Do you rent or own? Do you own a car? |
| **Arrest / Conviction** | Have you ever been convicted of a crime? (Only if directly related to the job.) | Have you ever been arrested? |

## 4. Offer Letter Process

Once you have identified your candidate and received formal hire approval, follow these steps to extend the offer.

### Step 1: Formal Offer Letter Request

Submit the offer letter request via email to People Ops. Include: candidate name, role, start date, compensation (base, variable if applicable), and any special terms discussed.

### Step 2: Verbal Offer

Once the Offer Letter Request has been approved by People Ops, the Hiring Manager should extend the verbal offer. Before making the call:

- Confirm the compensation, title, and start date.
  - Do not negotiate on the spot — if they push back on comp, say: *"Let me bring that back to the team and follow up."*
- Do not promise anything (equity, remote setup, bonus structure, flex arrangements) that has not been approved.
- Be ready to answer basic questions about benefits and next steps.

#### Verbal Offer Script

> "Hi [Candidate Name], do you have a few minutes? I have some great news.
>
> We'd love to officially offer you the [Job Title] position here at Appriss Retail. We were really impressed throughout the process, and we think you're going to be a fantastic addition to the team.
>
> Here are the details of the offer: Your start date would be [Date]. Your base salary would be [Amount] annually. You'd also be eligible for our annual bonus program, targeted at [X]% of your base salary, based on both company and individual performance.
>
> On the benefits side: you'd have access to medical, dental, and vision coverage starting your first day. We also offer a 401(k) with company match, unlimited PTO (salaried employees only), paid holidays, remote work, and various other stipends such as a gym/fitness reimbursement, access to the Calm Health app, and tuition reimbursement after you've been an employee for one year.
>
> You'll receive a formal offer letter from People Ops via our people system, Rippling, within the next day or two that lays all of this out in writing. That will be your chance to review everything in detail and ask any questions before signing. Be sure to check junk if you don't see it in the next few days.
>
> But I wanted to be the one to call and tell you first. We're really excited about this. Do you have any questions for me right now?"

### Step 3: Offer Letter Creation

1. People Ops will create the offer letter in Rippling. Allow 1–2 business days.
2. Review the draft — a copy routes to you before it goes to the candidate. Flag any corrections immediately.
3. Candidate signs electronically in Rippling. You will receive a confirmation email once signed.

### Start Date Rules

- **Minimum 2 weeks:** Start date must be at least 2 weeks from the date the candidate accepts.
- **1st or 16th only:** Start dates must fall on the 1st or 16th of the month to align with payroll cycles. If an exception needs to be made, connect with People Ops.

## 5. Onboarding Process

Once the offer is signed, People Ops handles the pre-Day 1 logistics.

### What People Ops Handles (Before Day 1)

| Item | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Background Check** | Initiated via First Advantage: ID verification, SSN trace, 7-year county criminal search, enhanced nationwide criminal database, DOJ sex offender registry, global sanctions, employment verification (3 employers, 7 years), education verification (highest degree). |
| **Rippling Onboarding** | New hire receives Rippling invitation and completes personal info, banking/direct deposit, tax forms, equipment preference, and policy acknowledgments. |
| **IT Provisioning** | Jira ticket automatically triggered once the new hire completes Rippling onboarding. Equipment ordered and system access provisioned. |
| **People Systems** | People Ops adds the new hire to Rippling, 15Five, Awardco, Navan, and all other applicable platforms. |
| **Pre-hire Emails** | Sent to the new hire at set intervals before Day 1. Contact People Ops for the current schedule. |

> **Contractors:** UnifyCX and Nuaav contractors are responsible for submitting completed background checks to People Ops directly. People Ops does not run background checks for external contractors.

### What You Need to Do (Before Day 1)

**Manager's Guide to Onboarding**

1. **Prepare their first week schedule.** Block time for your Day 1 intro meeting, team introductions, and a 30-minute check-in at end of week 1.
2. **Identify a buddy or onboarding contact.** Assign someone on the team who can answer day-to-day questions.
3. **Set up access requests in advance.** If the new hire needs tools or systems beyond standard provisioning, submit Jira IT tickets before Day 1.
4. **Be available.** Block your calendar for Day 1. New hires should not start to a manager who is unreachable.

Complete the **Onboarding Checklist** specific to your department.

### Day 1

**People Ops Meeting** *(Scheduled and led by People Ops)*
- Benefits review and enrollment
- Training requirements overview
- I-9 verification (employees only) — submitted to E-Verify via Rippling

**IT Meeting** *(Scheduled by People Ops and led by Trey)*
- Equipment confirmation
- System access check

**Compliance Training — Must Be Completed by Day 31**
- Information Security
- Harassment Prevention
- Anti-Bribery
- OWASP Training — if part of Engineering, Support, or Delivery departments

> **IMPORTANT:** Compliance training completion is tracked and required by Day 31. If an employee misses the deadline, flag it to People Ops immediately.

### 30/60/90 Day Milestones

Complete these in your customized Onboarding Checklist (see tabs 30, 60, and 90 Days for guidance).
